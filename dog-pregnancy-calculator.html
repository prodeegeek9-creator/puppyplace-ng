Key dates for your dam

    Dog pregnancy week by week

    Days are counted from the first mating. Every dam is a little different, so use these as a guide and follow your vet's advice.

    Signs of labour

    Normal temperature for a dog is 38.3–39.2°C. Take her rectal temperature morning and evening from about day 57. A drop to below about 37.2°C usually means labour within 12–24 hours.

    • Stage 1 (6–12 hours, sometimes up to 24): restlessness, panting, digging and nesting, shivering, refusing food, sometimes vomiting. The cervix is opening; you won't see straining yet.
    • Stage 2 (delivery): visible straining, then a puppy, often in its sac. Most puppies come 30–60 minutes apart, though a rest of up to 2 hours between puppies can be normal.
    • Stage 3: a placenta (afterbirth) follows each puppy. Count them; there should be one per puppy.

    🚨 Call your vet straight away if…

    • She strains hard for 30–60 minutes and no puppy comes.
    • More than 2 hours pass between puppies and you know more are inside.
    • Green or black discharge appears before the first puppy is born.
    • A puppy is stuck in the birth canal for more than 10–15 minutes.
    • She reaches day 68–70 from the first mating with no labour, or her temperature dropped more than 24 hours ago and nothing has happened.
    • Heavy bright-red bleeding, a foul-smelling discharge, fever above 39.5°C, or she seems very weak.
    • After whelping: trembling, stiffness, panting, wobbliness or fits. This can be eclampsia (low blood calcium), most common 1–4 weeks after birth. It is an emergency.
    • Hot, hard, red or painful teats (mastitis).

    Whelping kit checklist

    Have everything ready by about day 55.

    • Whelping box with low sides and a puppy rail
    • Plenty of clean towels and old bedsheets
    • Newspapers or puppy pads for the floor
    • Digital thermometer and lubricant
    • Clean, blunt scissors and unwaxed dental floss (to tie cords)
    • Iodine or chlorhexidine for the umbilical cords
    • Bulb syringe to clear puppies' noses and mouths
    • Kitchen scale to weigh puppies daily
    • Puppy milk replacer and feeding bottles, just in case
    • Heat pad or lamp, set up safely (puppies can't regulate their temperature at first)
    • Notebook to record birth times, weights and sex of each puppy
    • Your vet's phone number, and the nearest 24-hour clinic
    • Torch or rechargeable lamp in case of a power cut
    • Fuel for the generator, or charged inverter batteries

    Questions breeders ask

    How long is a dog pregnant?
    About 63 days from ovulation. Counted from mating, puppies usually arrive between day 58 and day 68, because sperm can live inside the female for several days, waiting for the eggs to be ready.
    Which mating date should I count from?
    Enter both the first and the last mating. The calculator counts the earliest likely date from the first mating and the latest from the last, which gives you a whelping window. If your vet did progesterone tests and knows the ovulation date, the due date is 63 days after ovulation and is accurate to about a day.
    When can a vet confirm she's pregnant?
    An ultrasound can see heartbeats from about day 25. A relaxin blood test works from about day 25 to 30. A vet can sometimes feel the puppies by palpation between days 26 and 35. An X-ray from day 55 shows the skeletons, so the puppies can be counted.
    When should I increase her food?
    Keep her on her normal amount for the first 4–5 weeks; overfeeding early only makes her fat, which makes birth harder. From about week 5, increase gradually by roughly 10% a week so that by whelping she eats about 25–50% more than usual, split into 3–4 smaller meals. While she's feeding the puppies she may need two to three times her normal amount.
    Should I give her calcium?
    Not during pregnancy unless your vet says so. Extra calcium before birth can stop her body from learning to release its own, which makes eclampsia more likely after whelping. A complete puppy or "all life stages" food has the right balance.
    Can she have her vaccines or tick treatment while pregnant?
    Bring her vaccinations up to date before mating. Avoid live vaccines during pregnancy. Ticks are a real risk in Nigeria, but not every tick or worm product is safe for pregnant dogs, so ask your vet which ones you can use.

    This calculator and guide are for general information only and are not a substitute for a veterinarian. Every pregnancy is different. Book a check-up with your vet early and contact them at once if you are worried about your dog.
